Real estate marketing uses "tax-free" for three mechanisms that behave nothing
alike. Learn the difference at Year 2, because every strategy decision after
this depends on which one is actually in play.

## Sheltered

**Depreciation** offsets rental income on paper while the property appreciates
in fact. The income was real; the tax bill is smaller. But depreciation is a
loan from your future self — it reduces your basis, and the IRS collects it
back as **recapture** when you sell. Unless you never sell (see below).

## Deferred

A **1031 exchange** rolls a gain into the next property untaxed. The tax is not
gone; it is riding along in your reduced basis, waiting for a sale that
triggers it.

## Never-taxed

The **step-up in basis** at transfer erases the deferred gain entirely. This is
the only genuinely tax-free mechanism of the three — and it only pays off if
you held the chain long enough to reach it.

Two honesty notes before the architecture. First, **each mechanism is earned,
not granted**: depreciation comes with tenants and roofs attached, 1031s come
with [45-day clocks and real deadline risk](/articles/1031-exchange-complete-guide),
and the step-up comes with the [discipline of holding through everything the
middle years throw at you](/articles/the-discipline-of-holding). "Tax-free" is
the reward side of a ledger whose cost side is work and time. Second, the rules
are policy, and policy is reviewed — the step-up has survived a century of
reform proposals, but a twenty-year plan should be robust to the parameters
drifting, which is one more argument for buying assets that work *before* their
tax treatment.

The whole twenty-year architecture of this site is these three words in order:
shelter while you hold, defer when you trade, step up when you transfer.
