**Are marinas a good investment?** They combine three of this site's favorite patterns on one shoreline: **frozen supply** (environmental permitting makes new marinas nearly impossible — the existing shoreline is the asset class, [billboard logic afloat](/articles/billboard-investing)), **land-lease economics** ([slips rent the water like lots rent the dirt](/articles/mobile-home-park-and-rv-park-investing) — boaters own the boats, you rent the space, tenancies run years), and **a fragmented mom-and-pop ownership base** now being [rolled up by institutional capital](/articles/institutional-real-estate-asset-classes). Revenue layers stack from wet slips through dry-rack storage to fuel, service, and ship stores — and the operating intensity scales with how many of those layers you run. It's a Years 9+ niche for operators graduating past [storage](/articles/self-storage-investing) who want the same scarcity math with water views.

**Wet slips** are the sticky core — annual leases, wait lists at desirable harbors, tenancy behavior like [lot tenants](/articles/mobile-home-park-and-rv-park-investing) (moving a boat's home port is a hassle owners avoid for years). **Dry-rack storage** is [self-storage with forklifts](/articles/self-storage-investing): higher density per shoreline foot, membership-like monthly billing, and a logistics operation (launch scheduling) as the service layer. **Fuel, service, and ship stores** convert the captive audience into margin — and the marina into a staffed hospitality-retail business. The honest sorting question is the [niche family's](/articles/niche-real-estate-operations) standard one: which layers are you buying, and which are you equipped to run?

## The consolidation arc

The sector is mid-way through the [familiar roll-up story](/articles/mobile-home-park-and-rv-park-investing): decades of family ownership, systematically under-priced slip rates, paper ledgers — now meeting institutional aggregators assembling marina portfolios for the same reasons they bought parks and [storage](/articles/self-storage-investing): scarce supply, sticky revenue, fragmented sellers. For the individual operator that means the classic two-sided opportunity: **buy from mom-and-pop** (under-market rates, unbilled amenities, no online booking — the value-add checklist writes itself) and **eventually sell to the platform** at portfolio pricing — or hold forever, because a permitted harbor with a wait list is about as [moated](/articles/niche-real-estate-operations) as cashflow gets. Financing runs [SBA for owner-operators](/articles/sba-loans-for-real-estate) (marinas are eligible, special-use tier) and community-bank commercial beyond; seller financing from retiring harbor families is common and worth asking for.

In the roadmap, marinas sit Years 9–15: an operations-heavy, scarcity-backed niche for investors with [systems competence](/cashflow/operations) and water access — and one of the few asset classes where the moat is literally enforced by the Army Corps of Engineers.
