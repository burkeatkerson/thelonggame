**How do you make money flipping land?** By trading in the one real estate market that still has no honest price tags: vacant land. There's no Zestimate for a 5-acre parcel, no comps that fit, and hundreds of thousands of absentee owners who inherited dirt they've never seen and quietly resent paying taxes on. Offer them 30–50 cents on the dollar of real value — often the first offer they've ever received — and resell to the buyers who *do* value it: neighbors, builders, hunters, dreamers. No tenants, no toilets, no rehab. The margins embarrass houses; the trade-off is a slower, thinner buyer pool, which the pros solve with [seller financing](/articles/seller-financing-explained) — and the subdivide play multiplies everything.

## The subdivide multiplier

The cleanest value-add in real estate needs no contractor: **one parcel in, several lots out**. A 10-acre parcel worth $80k whole often sells as five 2-acre lots at $30–35k each — $150k+ from a survey, a plat application, and municipal patience. The ladder of intensity: **simple splits** (many rural counties allow 2–4 way splits administratively — weeks, not years), **minor subdivisions** (survey + plat approval + maybe a shared drive), and **major subdivisions with road/utility work** — which is [development](/articles/real-estate-development-complete-guide), a different business with different capital. The skill is knowing your county's split rules *before* buying: parcels purchased at whole-parcel pricing with by-right split potential are the land flipper's version of [buying under the ARV](/articles/house-flipping-strategies-complete-guide). Above this sits the full [entitlement play](/articles/real-estate-development-complete-guide) — rezone, approve, sell the paper — where returns and binary risk both go vertical.

Every land discount exists because most buyers can't price these binaries. You can:

An afternoon of checks per parcel, mostly free, mostly public — [the same records muscle as every off-market channel](/articles/finding-off-market-and-distressed-properties). The flippers who lose money in land are, almost without exception, the ones who skipped a line on this table.

## Where land flipping fits

It's a Years 1–4 capital engine with the lowest entry ticket in the pillar — first deals close at $5–20k all-in, [tax-sale channels](/articles/tax-lien-vs-tax-deed-investing) feed inventory, and [land wholesaling](/articles/wholesaling-real-estate-complete-guide) runs the same funnel with no capital at all. The graduation paths fork: volume flippers become **note holders** (the terms-sale portfolio quietly becoming [a paper business](/articles/note-investing-and-private-lending-guide)), split specialists climb toward [entitlements and development](/articles/real-estate-development-complete-guide), and patient ones migrate into [income-producing land holds](/articles/land-investing-for-passive-income). All three started with the same letter to the same forgotten owner.
