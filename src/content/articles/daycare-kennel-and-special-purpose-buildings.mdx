**Why do investors buy daycare and kennel buildings?** For the stickiest tenancy math in small commercial: a licensed daycare's permit, state-mandated build-out (fencing, ratios, outdoor play area), and — decisively — its *enrolled families* are all welded to the address. Moving means re-licensing, rebuilding, and asking a hundred families to change their morning routine; so operators renew, and renew, and renew. The same physics govern **veterinary clinics and boarding kennels** (special-use zoning + equipment + clientele), **dental and medical suites** ([plumbed-in equipment as tenant glue](/articles/small-commercial-real-estate-investing)), and the broader family of **special-purpose buildings**: the landlord's product isn't space — it's *licensed continuity*, and tenants pay above-market rents on long terms to keep it. This is the [operations-premium family's](/articles/niche-real-estate-operations) purest landlord seat: all of the niche stickiness, none of the operating job.

The pricing consequence: occupied special-purpose buildings trade at 7–8.5% cap rates — above [generic NNN retail](/articles/nnn-lease-investing) — precisely because the market prices the single-use risk. The buyer's edge is realizing the *stickiness* is underpriced relative to the *specificity*: a daycare fifteen years into an address with full enrollment is closer to [ground-lease-grade durability](/articles/ground-lease-investing) than to speculative single-tenant risk — if the operator's business is sound, which is exactly what the diligence checks.

## The seats and the sector notes

As everywhere in [the niche family](/articles/niche-real-estate-operations): **landlord** (the seat this page prices — premium rent, licensed continuity, zero operations), **operator** ([SBA-financed at ~10% down](/articles/sba-loans-for-real-estate) buying your own facility — the daycare or kennel owner's classic wealth move, converting rent into equity), or **both via PropCo/OpCo** ([the standard split](/articles/real-estate-tax-strategy-complete-guide)). Sector currents worth knowing: childcare is a permanent shortage industry with public funding tailwinds (and ratio/regulation shifts as the watch-item), **pet care is the recession-proof compounder** (boarding, daycare, and vet demand grew through every downturn on record — and [institutional consolidators are rolling up vet practices](/articles/institutional-real-estate-asset-classes), making their landlords' credit *better* over time), and neighborhood **medical migration** keeps converting retail-adjacent buildings into [MOB-lite assets](/articles/small-commercial-real-estate-investing). In the roadmap this is Years 8–14 material: the small-commercial graduate's specialty lane, where an afternoon of license-reading buys tenancy durability the generic-box buyer never gets.
