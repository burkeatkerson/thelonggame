**Can you buy commercial real estate with an SBA loan?** Yes — if your *business occupies it*: SBA 504 and 7(a) loans finance owner-occupied commercial property at **~10% down** (versus 25–35% conventional), with long amortizations and, in the 504's case, fixed rates on a large slice of the stack. The occupancy rule (your operating business must use 51%+ of an existing building) is the gate — and the strategy: for the [operations-heavy niches this site maps](/articles/niche-real-estate-operations) — [self-storage](/articles/self-storage-investing), [laundromats](/articles/laundromat-investing), [car washes](/articles/car-wash-investing), [small hotels](/articles/boutique-hotel-investing), daycares, kennels — the SBA is the [financing ladder's](/articles/real-estate-financing-ladder) commercial house hack: the government subsidizing operators into owning their real estate, at leverage investors can't touch.

Underwriting runs on the *business*: historical or projected cash flow covering debt service (typically 1.15–1.25×), your experience, and the building's role in it — which is why SBA credit reaches deals conventional CRE lending prices away: the first-time [motel buyer](/articles/boutique-hotel-investing) with hospitality experience, the [laundromat acquisition](/articles/laundromat-investing) with cash-flow history, the daycare operator expanding into an owned building. Special-use properties (hotels, washes, kennels) that terrify banks are routine SBA fare — at the 15% down tier, still less than half of conventional's ask.

The honest cost ledger: guarantee fees (2–3%ish, financeable), closing timelines of 45–90 days (SBA-fluent lenders — "Preferred Lenders" — compress this; use one), **full personal guarantees** from 20%+ owners (non-negotiable — [the non-recourse graduation](/articles/real-estate-financing-ladder) happens elsewhere, later), life-insurance requirements on key principals in many deals, and prepayment structures (the 504 debenture's declining penalty over 10 years) that make these hold-the-building loans, not flip vehicles. Against all of it: the 15–25 percentage points of down payment you didn't part with, compounding in your next acquisition.

In the roadmap, SBA lending is the Years 6–12 operator's instrument — the bridge from [running a business on someone's real estate](/articles/rental-arbitrage-and-real-estate-side-businesses) to owning the building it runs in, and the reason the [operations-heavy pillar niches](/articles/niche-real-estate-operations) are reachable at savings-account scale. The government wants Main Street owning its property; the 10% down is the policy working as intended.

Conventional lenders finance buildings; the SBA finances the businesses standing in them — at leverage that treats an operator's competence as the down payment it effectively is.
