**Why is everyone buying car washes?** Because the express tunnel converted a weather-dependent cash business into a *subscription* business: unlimited-wash memberships at $20–45/month now drive 60–80% of revenue at modern tunnels — recurring, predictable, churn-managed like software — layered on prime retail real estate. That combination pulled private equity into a full [roll-up wave](/articles/institutional-real-estate-asset-classes), which changed the game for individual investors on both sides: single sites now trade at premium multiples (the exit is rich), and the mom-and-pop tunnels that haven't converted to membership models are the sector's [classic value-add](/articles/niche-real-estate-operations). This is the map: formats, the membership math, and the underwriting that separates a subscription machine from an expensive robot in a flood zone.

The operating skill is genuinely subscription management: sign-up conversion at the pay station, churn saves, plan-tier upsells, and member experience (line speed is retention). A tunnel at 35% membership penetration and one at 70% are different businesses on the same corner — which is precisely the [value-add spread](/articles/small-commercial-real-estate-investing) buyers hunt: acquire the under-membered wash, install the playbook, and the revenue re-rates without pouring concrete.

The self-serve note deserves emphasis for this site's readers: aging self-serve washes sit on exactly the hard corners [retail developers want](/articles/real-estate-development-complete-guide), and often trade at yields that pay you to wait for the [land's next life](/articles/land-investing-for-passive-income) — income-producing land banking disguised as a coin-op business.

## Underwriting a wash

Site first: 25,000+ daily traffic counts, easy in/easy out (one awkward median kills volume), visibility, and rooftops-plus-employment demographics — [the same corner logic as every retail asset](/articles/small-commercial-real-estate-investing). Then the wash: membership penetration and churn (the growth lever), equipment age against the ~10-year refresh cycle ($500k–1M — the reserve line, not a surprise), water/sewer rates and reclaim systems (utilities vary wildly by municipality), competition radius (tunnels cannibalize within 3–5 miles, and the roll-ups are building aggressively), and — always — **the real estate**: own the corner or control it long, because the [captive-infrastructure logic](/articles/laundromat-investing) applies doubled to a building with a 130-foot tunnel through it. Financing runs the familiar路径: [SBA for owner-operators](/articles/real-estate-financing-ladder) at ~10% down, conventional commercial beyond, and the roll-ups paying cash at multiples that make longtime operators' retirement plans.

In the roadmap this is a Years 8–14 asset: heavier operations than [storage](/articles/self-storage-investing), richer than almost anything at comparable scale, and carrying a live strategic kicker — **the consolidation wave is the exit**: build or fix two or three tunnels with real membership books and clean numbers, and the aggregators' appetite converts your operating years into [portfolio-multiple pricing](/articles/institutional-real-estate-asset-classes).
