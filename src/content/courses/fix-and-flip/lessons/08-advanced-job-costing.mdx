The walk-through estimate got you under contract. Now it graduates into the most underrated document in renovation: the **scope of work** — every line item with location, spec, quantity, and price, organized by trade in build order. This one document is simultaneously your bid sheet, your contract exhibit, your draw schedule, and your post-project report card. Flippers who write real scopes control their projects; flippers who don't fund their contractors' learning curves.

## Anatomy of a real line item

Vague: *"Update kitchen — $18,000."*
Real: *"KITCHEN — Demo existing cabinets/counters, haul (1 dumpster share). Install 14 LF shaker cabinets, allowance $210/LF installed. 32 sqft quartz counters @ $68/sqft installed. Subway tile backsplash, 28 sqft @ $14 installed. GE slate appliance package, allowance $3,400. Sink/faucet allowance $650. — $17,850."*

The difference isn't pedantry — it's *biddability*. Three contractors can price the second version identically-scoped, which means their bids finally mean something (Lesson 9). And the **allowances** — dollar caps on every selection ("faucet allowance $650") — are the clause that keeps your finish choices from quietly adding $9k, because every "we'll pick the tile later" is an unpriced liability with your name on it.

Each stage becomes a **draw milestone** — money released when the stage passes *your* walk — which is Lesson 9's whole payment architecture, pre-built by this document.

## The variance file: how estimates get sharp

The scope's final job happens after closing: **actual vs. estimate, line by line.** Kitchen ran $2,100 over (tile labor), roof came in $900 under, the "forgotten ten" ran exactly as forgotten as ever. Five projects of variance data and your walk-through numbers converge within 10% — which changes your *offers*: you can bid closer to true MAO than competitors carrying fear-padding in every line. Your variance file is proprietary market data nobody can buy. Software helps (FlipperForce-class tools, or a disciplined spreadsheet with our structure); the habit is the asset. → [The estimating stack, all levels](/articles/estimating-rehab-costs)

**Two rules from the graduates:** walk the house with your contractor *before* the budget becomes a contract — their eyes catch what checklists can't — and **never let contingency migrate into scope**. Contingency is insurance for the wall you haven't opened; upgrade creep dressed as "while we're at it" is how 15% margins become 6%.
